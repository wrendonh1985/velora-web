Velora Privacy Policy

Last updated: 5 October 2026

Velora is an app for tracking personal and household finances. This policy explains what data we handle, what for, who it is shared with, and how you can control it. It is written to be understood, not to protect us.

Who is responsible

The data controller is Wilson Rendón, an individual residing in Colombia.

Domicile: Medellín, Colombia (postal code 050034).

For any question, complaint, or to exercise your rights, write to velora.privacidad@gmail.com.

What data we handle

CategoryWhat it includesWhy
Account Your email address and password. The password is always stored hashed (bcrypt), never in plain text; nobody, including us, can read it. If you choose to sign in with Google, instead of a password we store the identifier Google gives us for that account together with the verified email address that comes with it. We do not receive your Google password, nor access to your mail, your contacts, or anything else in your Google account. Profile Your chosen display name, your role in the household, and your theme preference (light or dark). The language you pick stays on your phone only: it never reaches us. So the app works and looks the way you want.
Financial data you enter Transactions (date, amount, description, category, notes), budget, fixed income and recurring expenses, debts with their installments, insurance charges and the extra principal payments you make, savings goals with their target, target date and the plan of how much you mean to set aside each month, and investments with what you put in and the value you record yourself. Accounts and cards The name you give them (your bank, for example) and what type it is: account, cash, digital wallet, foreign currency, interest account, allowance or card. To know where each transaction comes from or lands in, and which statement each card purchase falls on.
Household The household name, its currency, and who belongs to it. To share the app with your family, if you choose to.

Technical error log Which operation failed, when, and the technical error code. We do not store the full error text, because it could carry along data you had typed. So we can fix failures.

What Velora does not do

  • No analytics, no advertising, no third-party trackers. There is no SDK following what you do inside or outside the app.
  • We do not sell or hand over your data to anyone, for commercial or any other purposes.
  • We do not connect to your bank. Velora does not read your accounts or bank transactions: everything you see there, you typed.
  • We do not use your data to train models or to profile you.

Your fingerprint and your face

If you turn on biometric unlock, the one verifying your fingerprint or face is your own phone, using the manufacturer's system. Velora never receives, sees or stores any biometric data. All we store is a yes/no of whether you enabled that option.

Who else can see your data

If you belong to a household with other people, whoever administers it can see the detail of your transactions: how much you spend, on what and when. The other members cannot.

The only exception is a shared savings goal. When someone shares a goal with you, everyone taking part sees how much each person has put in: only the total contributed to that goal, never the detail of your transactions or their dates. You see the same about them. Beyond that figure, the rest of your records stays yours alone.

That is why, before joining a household by invitation, the app warns you and you have to accept it explicitly. If you do not accept, you do not join. The date you accepted is recorded.

Another person in the household can create a transaction in your history when they split a loan installment, an extra principal payment or their card statement between several people: each person's share is recorded in their own history. Only the owner of that debt or card can do it, only with people in your own household, and you can delete that transaction from your transactions if that is not how it went. Nobody can touch the rest of your records.

If you are the only person in your household, nobody else sees your data.

Who we share it with

If you join a parche, the people in it see your name and what is recorded inside it (the expenses, your share and the payments between you). They do not see your accounts, your transactions or anything about your household. Anyone with the invite code sees the parche's name and the name of whoever created it. What you confirm from a parche becomes one more transaction in your Velora, so your household's admin sees it just like your other transactions.

If you add someone who doesn't have the app, we store the name you type and whatever is recorded under that name in the parche, and only the people in the parche see it. If that person joins with their link, it becomes theirs and they see it in their Velora. If they don't join, it stays in the parche because it is part of its accounts: anyone in the parche can change the name, and that person can ask us to remove it by writing to velora.privacidad@gmail.com. If they already have expenses or payments recorded, those figures stay because without them the others' accounts would not add up, but we delete their name and they appear as "Deleted person", with nothing that identifies them.

Only with those needed for the app to work, and always as data processors, not owners of the data:

  • Supabase Pte. Ltd. — hosts the database and the account system.
  • Brevo (Sendinblue SAS) — sends the account emails: the one to confirm your address and the one to recover your password. It receives your email address and the content of that message; nothing else.
  • Microsoft (OneDrive) — stores our encrypted backups. Without our key, Microsoft cannot read them.
  • Google Play and the App Store — distribute the application. They do not receive your financial data.
  • Google LLC — only if you choose "Continue with Google". It checks that the account is yours and returns your verified email address; from then on Google knows you have a Velora account. It receives none of your financial data. If you sign in with email and password, Google plays no part at all, and the app ships no Google component inside it: the check happens in your phone's browser.
  • Apple Inc. — only if you choose "Sign in with Apple". It checks that the account is yours and returns your name and email (or a hidden one that forwards to you). It receives none of your financial data. When you delete your account we ask it to withdraw the permission it gave us.

We may also hand over data if a competent authority legally requires it.

Where your data lives

On Supabase servers in the United States (region us-east-2, Ohio). If you write from Colombia, the European Union or another country, your data is transferred and stored there. Supabase acts as a processor and is bound by a data processing agreement, which is part of its terms of service. Brevo processes your email address in the European Union.

On your phone

Velora keeps a copy of your data on the device itself so the app can open and be useful offline: broadly, everything you had already looked at inside the app —movements, balances, accounts and cards, and also debts, goals, investments and budget—, along with your name, your household and your role. A movement you write down with no signal stays there until there is a connection to send it, even if you close the app.

That copy lives in the app's private storage, which other apps on the phone cannot reach, but it is not encrypted: anyone able to unlock your phone could get to it. It is deleted in full when you sign out. Your session — the keys that identify your account — is stored encrypted, in the iOS Keychain or the Android Keystore.

There is something that exists only on your phone and never reaches our servers: the calculations you save in the calculator, under the names you give them. We do not see them, they do not travel anywhere and they are not shared with the other people in your household, not even if they use the same account from another phone. For the same reason, they do not appear when you export your data and there is nothing to delete when you delete your account: we do not have them. They are deleted when you sign out and when you uninstall the app.

Payments

Velora is free today. When a paid subscription exists, the charge will be processed entirely by Google Play or the App Store: we never receive or see your card details. We will update this policy before enabling it, to spell out who is involved.

How long we keep it

For as long as you have an account. When you delete it from the app:

  • Your email, password, transactions, budget, fixed income and recurring expenses, accounts, cards, transfers, debts, goals with their contribution plan, planned entries, balance corrections and investments are deleted.
  • If you were the only person in the household, the whole household is deleted.
  • If other people remain, shared items (the household categories) stay for them; yours do not.
  • With one exception: if you put money into an extra principal payment on someone else's debt, that transaction is not deleted. It is left without an owner and stops being linked to you. It is the only way that person's debt still adds up: without it, their balance would show as reduced with no payment to explain it.

Deletion is immediate and cannot be undone. The only thing left is what was already in backups: ours, which we make every week and keep on our own computer and, encrypted, in the cloud; and those the database provider makes, which it rotates within days. They are only used to restore the service if something fails, and none is kept longer than eight weeks: after that, no copy with your data is left. The files produced by Export my data delete themselves in under a day.

Deleting your account is never blocked. If you are the only person administering a household where other people remain, Velora makes the longest-standing member an administrator and goes ahead with the deletion: nobody gets stuck, and the household is not left without anyone to administer it.

You can also request it from outside the app, on this page.

Your rights

At any time you can:

  • Access your data and take it with you: in the app, under Settings → Export my data, which hands you everything as CSV files.
  • Correct it: by editing your transactions and your profile.
  • Delete it: under Settings → Delete my account.
  • Object to processing or withdraw your consent, by writing to us.

None of these rights costs money or requires a justification. If you prefer to exercise them by email, write to velora.privacidad@gmail.com.

How to make a query or a complaint. Write to velora.privacidad@gmail.com from your account's email address (or, if you have no account, telling us how to identify you) and tell us what you are asking for: to know your data, correct it, delete it, withdraw your consent or complain about something. If it is a complaint, describe the facts behind it and attach any documents that support it.

In Colombia, under Law 1581 of 2012, we will answer queries within ten (10) business days; if we cannot, we will tell you why and answer within five (5) more at most. Complaints within fifteen (15) business days, extendable by eight (8) more with notice of the reason. If your complaint is missing something, we will ask for it within the following five (5) business days; if two (2) months go by without you completing it, we will consider it withdrawn. While we resolve it, your data will be flagged as "complaint in progress". If you are not satisfied, you may turn to the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio. If you are in the European Union, we will respond within the one (1) month period set by the GDPR and you may complain to your country's supervisory authority.

Children

Velora is for people aged 18 or over. A minor may only use it with the authorisation of their mother, father or legal guardian, who gives it on their behalf. The same applies to a minor someone adds to a parche without the app: whoever adds them must have that authorisation. If you believe we hold a minor's data without it, write to us and we will delete it.

Security

All traffic is encrypted (HTTPS). Passwords are stored with bcrypt. Access to data is isolated per person and per household directly in the database, so the application cannot read another household's data even if it tried. No system is infallible: if a breach affecting you were to occur, we will tell you, and we will report it to the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio within fifteen (15) business days of detecting it.

Changes to this policy

If anything relevant changes, we will update this page and the date above, and we will let you know inside the app when the change affects how we handle your data.
